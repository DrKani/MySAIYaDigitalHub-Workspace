# SSIOM History

## The Beginning

In the early 1980s, a small but deeply committed group of Malaysians — inspired by the life and teachings of Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba — came together with a shared conviction: that the values Baba embodied could transform not just individual lives, but the fabric of Malaysian society.

On **October 8, 1983**, this conviction took formal shape. The organisation was registered as **Majlis Pusat Sathya Sai Baba Malaysia** — the Sathya Sai Baba Central Council of Malaysia. It was, from day one, an interfaith endeavour: Malaysians of different ethnicities and religious backgrounds uniting under the banner of love and selfless service.

---

## Four Decades of Growth

Through the late 1980s and 1990s, Sai Centres began to emerge across the peninsula — in Selangor, Kuala Lumpur, Perak, Penang, Negeri Sembilan, Johor, and beyond. Each centre became a local hub for bhajan sessions, service projects, children's spiritual education, and community care.

The organisation grew not through recruitment drives or marketing campaigns but through something simpler and more durable: the visible transformation of its members. People noticed something different about those who spent time in Sai Centres — a quietness, a generosity, a groundedness that proved more persuasive than any advertisement.

---

## 2014: A Unified Identity

On **March 4, 2014**, the organisation was formally renamed the **Sathya Sai International Organisation of Malaysia (SSIOM)** — aligning with the international parent body, the **Sri Sathya Sai International Organisation (SSSIO)**, which coordinates the Sai movement across 150+ countries.

This was more than a name change. It represented a deepened sense of global belonging — an acknowledgment that the work being done in Petaling Jaya or Ipoh was part of a larger, worldwide movement inspired by the same source.

---

## Today: Robust, Relevant, and Rooted

Today, SSIOM operates **54 active Sai Centres** across Malaysia, serving over **2,000 registered active members**. Its programmes span spiritual development, social service, children's education, youth leadership, and cultural preservation — all free of charge, all without condition.

In the past decade in particular, SSIOM has evolved into a robust learning platform, preserving its traditional values while adapting fluidly to the needs of a rapidly changing Malaysian society. The addition of the MySAIYa digital hub is simply the latest chapter in that story of thoughtful evolution.
