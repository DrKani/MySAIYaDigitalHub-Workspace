# His Life

## A Life That Began in Puttaparthi

Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba was born Sathya Narayana Raju on 23 November 1926 in Puttaparthi, in what is today Andhra Pradesh, India.

The Puttaparthi of His childhood was a small rural community.

The world into which He was born was very different from the world He would leave eighty-four years later.

India remained under British rule.

International travel was inaccessible to most people.

Mass communication was limited.

The idea that a remote village might one day receive visitors from across the world would have seemed improbable.

Yet Puttaparthi would eventually become internationally known as the home of Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba and the centre of a worldwide spiritual and humanitarian movement.

---

## The Early Years

Accounts of Sathya's childhood consistently describe unusual qualities of compassion, generosity, spiritual inclination and creativity.

He showed concern for people and animals.

He shared food with those in need.

He demonstrated interests in music, devotional singing, drama and poetry.

As a child, He gathered other children for devotional activities and formed a group known as the Pandhari Bhajan group.

These early accounts are significant not simply because they describe an unusual child.

They introduce themes that would later define His public mission:

Devotion.

Compassion.

Education.

Creativity.

Service.

And the ability to bring people together around a higher purpose.

---

## 1940: The Declaration

In 1940, during His teenage years, Sathya Narayana Raju made the declaration that would mark the beginning of His public mission.

He announced that He was Sai Baba and that His work and devotees were calling Him.

Soon afterwards, people began gathering around the young Sathya Sai Baba.

Among the devotional songs associated with this period was *Manasa Bhajare Guru Charanam* — an invitation to the mind to contemplate the feet of the Guru as the means of crossing the difficult ocean of worldly existence.

The boy from Puttaparthi had stepped into a public spiritual mission that would continue for more than seven decades.

---

## A Mission Takes Shape

The early years of Bhagawan's mission were comparatively simple.

Devotees gathered around Him.

Bhajans were sung.

Spiritual questions were asked.

Visitors sought guidance.

Accounts of extraordinary experiences and phenomena spread.

Increasing numbers of people travelled to Puttaparthi.

But even as attention grew around His person, Bhagawan consistently directed people towards transformation.

Devotion was to become character.

Faith was to become courage.

Spiritual understanding was to become action.

Love was to become service.

The movement that grew around Him therefore developed not only through gatherings and devotional practice, but increasingly through organised spiritual, educational and humanitarian activity.

---

## 1950: Prasanthi Nilayam

On 23 November 1950, Prasanthi Nilayam was inaugurated in Puttaparthi.

Its name means the **Abode of Supreme Peace**.

Over time, the ashram developed into the spiritual centre of Bhagawan's mission.

People arrived from across India.

Later, from around the world.

They came from different religions, professions, cultures and social backgrounds.

Some came seeking spiritual guidance.

Some came because of personal difficulties.

Some were drawn by accounts of Bhagawan's life.

Others came simply to understand.

At Prasanthi Nilayam, visitors encountered a rhythm of devotional, spiritual and educational activity centred upon the possibility of inner transformation.

The remote village of Bhagawan's childhood gradually became a meeting place for humanity.

---

## The Organisation Develops

As the number of devotees and service activities grew, organisational structures developed to support spiritual practice, service and education.

The Sathya Sai movement expanded across India and internationally.

Sai Centres and Groups brought people together for devotional singing, spiritual study, service and educational programmes.

Yet Bhagawan repeatedly emphasised that organisation was a means, not the destination.

The value of spiritual activity lay in transformation.

The purpose of service was purification of the heart.

The purpose of education was character.

The purpose of devotion was the experience of the Divine.

An organisation bearing His name therefore carried a particular responsibility:

Its values had to become visible in the conduct of its members.

---

## Education and the Formation of Character

Education became one of the defining dimensions of Bhagawan's mission.

He challenged approaches to education concerned only with information, employment and material success.

Knowledge was important.

Excellence was important.

But without character, education remained incomplete.

Schools and colleges were established under His guidance.

The Sri Sathya Sai Institute of Higher Learning developed as a university integrating academic excellence, discipline, service and values-based education.

Bhagawan described the ideal of **Educare** — drawing out the Human Values already inherent within the individual.

Education was not simply preparation for earning a living.

It was preparation for living well.

---

## Healthcare as Compassion in Action

Bhagawan's vision of healthcare similarly challenged conventional assumptions.

Why, He asked through action, should high-quality medical care become inaccessible because a person is poor?

Healthcare institutions established under His guidance provided treatment without charging patients.

General hospitals served local communities.

Super-speciality hospitals offered advanced medical and surgical care.

The principle was both practical and spiritual.

A person in suffering should be treated with dignity.

The ability to pay should not determine the value of a human life.

Medicine could become an expression of love.

---

## Water and Human Dignity

Bhagawan's humanitarian work also addressed one of the most fundamental human needs:

Safe drinking water.

Large-scale drinking-water projects were undertaken to serve communities facing chronic water scarcity.

These projects demonstrated another characteristic of His work.

Spirituality was not separated from infrastructure.

Compassion could require engineering.

Love could require planning.

Service could require technical excellence, administration, resources and long-term commitment.

A spiritual vision, when translated into action, could address the practical realities of human suffering.

---

## A Worldwide Community

Over the decades, increasing numbers of people from outside India encountered Bhagawan and His teachings.

Sai Centres and organisations developed in countries around the world.

Different cultures expressed devotion differently.

Different communities faced different needs.

But several principles remained constant:

Respect all religions.

Serve without seeking reward.

Develop character.

Practise the Human Values.

See humanity as one family.

And recognise the divine nature inherent within every person.

The worldwide movement did not require people to become culturally identical.

Its deeper aspiration was unity within diversity.

---

## The Teacher and the Individual

The scale of Bhagawan's public work can make it easy to overlook another dimension of His life.

The individual.

Accounts from devotees frequently describe personal encounters in which Bhagawan remembered circumstances, offered guidance, corrected behaviour, encouraged service or provided comfort.

For many, the relationship was intensely personal.

Yet even this personal attention pointed towards a larger purpose.

Bhagawan did not merely seek followers who depended permanently upon His physical presence.

He continually directed people towards inner strength, self-confidence, self-discipline and recognition of the Divine within.

The outer relationship was meant to awaken an inner journey.

---

## The Final Years

Bhagawan continued His public mission into the later years of His life.

By then, Puttaparthi had changed dramatically from the village of His childhood.

Institutions had grown.

The international movement had expanded.

Generations of devotees had encountered His teachings.

Yet His central message remained remarkably consistent.

Love.

Service.

Character.

Unity.

Self-transformation.

The divinity inherent within all.

On 24 April 2011, Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba left His physical form.

For the worldwide Sai community, it marked the end of an extraordinary period of direct physical presence.

But it did not mark the end of His mission.

---

## A Life That Continues Through Action

How should a life such as Bhagawan's be remembered?

Through dates?

Institutions?

Photographs?

Personal experiences?

Teachings?

All are important.

But Bhagawan repeatedly placed responsibility upon the individual.

His life was not offered merely for admiration.

It was an example and an invitation.

The hospital asks:

**How can compassion become practical?**

The university asks:

**What is education without character?**

The water project asks:

**What responsibility do we have towards the suffering of others?**

The interfaith message asks:

**Can we remain rooted in our own faith while respecting another person's path?**

The Human Values ask:

**Are our beliefs visible in the way we live?**

And His spiritual teaching asks the deepest question of all:

**Who am I?**

The continuing legacy of Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba is therefore not found only in what He built.

It is found wherever a person chooses to transform love into service, knowledge into character and spiritual understanding into daily life.
