## Who Is He?

From a small, forgotten village that did not even appear on the maps of independent India, arose a presence that would touch almost every corner of the globe.

Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba was born Sathya Narayana Raju on **23 November 1926** in Puttaparthi, a quiet agrarian hamlet in pre-independence India. He lived among us until **24 April 2011** — 85 years that would quietly and irrevocably change millions of lives across every continent.

To those who encountered Him, He was many things at once: a living embodiment of unconditional love, a master educator, a humanitarian of extraordinary scale, and above all, a mirror — reflecting back to each person the truth of who they really are.

Ask a Sai devotee to describe their first encounter with Baba, and watch something shift in their face. Some speak of a gaze that seemed to look straight through the surface of life. Others recall a smile that dissolved years of worry in an instant. Many simply say: "I felt seen. Completely understood. Completely loved." Not because He performed a miracle — though miracles are certainly part of His story — but because He saw the divine in them before they could see it in themselves.

---

## His Central Message

In a world complicated by philosophies and debates, Sathya Sai Baba's message arrived with childlike clarity:

> "Love All, Serve All. Help Ever, Hurt Never."

He did not preach a religion. He did not build a denomination. He pointed every person — regardless of faith, culture, or background — back to the same essential truth:

> "Yes, I am God — and so are you. The only difference is that I know it."

This was not arrogance. It was an invitation. An invitation to stop underselling yourself. To recognise that the divine is not distant, not reserved for temples or scholars, but resident — quietly, patiently — within every human heart.

## Why He Matters to MySAIYa

We at MySAIYa are not a fan club. We are a community of young adults who have chosen to take that invitation seriously.

The life of Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba is not ancient history to us — it is a living blueprint. His insistence that spirituality must be practical, that love must be expressed through action, that character is built not in lecture halls but in everyday choices — these are the principles that animate everything we do: every service project, every study circle, every act of quiet kindness.

He remains, for us, the most compelling answer to the most urgent question any young person faces: *How do I live a life that actually means something?*
