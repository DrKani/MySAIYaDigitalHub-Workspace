# Our Shared Commitments

## More Than Rules

Every meaningful community needs a shared understanding of how its members walk together.

For MySAIYa, this understanding begins with values.

We are a diverse community of young adults.

We come from different backgrounds.

Different cultures.

Different professions.

Different personalities.

Different levels of spiritual understanding and experience.

We will not always think alike.

We will not always agree.

And none of us arrives as a finished product.

What allows us to grow together is a shared commitment to conduct ourselves with love, respect, responsibility and awareness.

These guidelines express that commitment.

They are not intended to create a culture of policing.

They exist to protect the spirit of our community.

To help us remember how we wish to treat one another.

And to translate the values we speak about into the way we actually live, serve, communicate and work together.

---

## We Are Spiritual Aspirants

MySAIYa is a community of people learning and growing together.

We do not expect perfection from one another.

We do expect sincerity.

Every young adult is at a different stage of life and spiritual development.

Some may have grown up within the Sai Organisation.

Others may be newly discovering the teachings of Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba.

Some may be deeply involved.

Others may participate occasionally.

Some may feel spiritually confident.

Others may arrive with questions.

There is room for all sincere seekers.

Our responsibility is not to compare journeys.

It is to support growth.

---

## We Treat Every Person With Dignity

Human dignity is fundamental to the MySAIYa community.

We seek to treat every person with respect regardless of:

- ethnicity;
- language;
- culture;
- socioeconomic background;
- profession;
- education;
- physical ability;
- personality;
- level of organisational involvement; or
- spiritual experience.

No one should feel that they must become more important, more accomplished, more connected or more visible before they deserve kindness and respect.

Every person matters.

---

## We Respect Malaysia's Multi-Religious Society

MySAIYa exists within the diverse religious and cultural context of Malaysia.

SSIOM is an interfaith spiritual organisation inspired by the teachings of Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba.

We respect the world's major religions and the principle that every sincere spiritual path can lead a person towards greater love, character and understanding.

MySAIYa does not seek to convert anyone from one religion to another.

We uphold the principle of non-proselytisation.

We encourage individuals to deepen their spiritual lives in accordance with their own faith traditions while respecting the religious sensitivities and legal framework of Malaysia.

Our programmes and communications should consistently reflect this respect.

---

## We Place Sai at the Centre

MySAIYa exists because of the life, message, works and teachings of Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba.

Our activities may be contemporary.

Our methods may evolve.

Our programmes may be creative.

But our spiritual foundation remains constant.

We therefore seek to ensure that our work reflects the values He taught:

**Truth.**

**Right Conduct.**

**Peace.**

**Love.**

**Non-violence.**

Placing Sai at the centre does not mean mentioning His name repeatedly.

It means asking whether the way we work reflects the values we claim to represent.

---

## We Serve Without Seeking Reward

Selfless service is central to the MySAIYa journey.

We serve because service helps others.

But service also changes us.

It teaches humility.

Expands awareness.

Challenges prejudice.

Develops gratitude.

And reminds us that our abilities can be used for purposes larger than ourselves.

We therefore seek to serve without unnecessary attention, competition or expectation of recognition.

Photographs, reports and communication may be important for accountability, awareness and inspiration.

But the dignity of the people we serve must always come before the promotion of the service we perform.

---

## We Respect the Dignity of Those We Serve

People are never projects.

They are never content.

They are never opportunities for us to appear compassionate.

When participating in service activities, we seek to protect:

- personal dignity;
- privacy;
- informed consent;
- cultural sensitivity;
- safety; and
- appropriate boundaries.

We avoid language or imagery that humiliates, stereotypes or exploits individuals and communities.

Service must be offered with respect.

---

## We Speak With Care

Words can strengthen a community.

They can also damage one.

We therefore seek to communicate with honesty, kindness and responsibility.

This includes:

- avoiding gossip;
- refusing to spread unverified information;
- addressing disagreements constructively;
- giving feedback respectfully;
- avoiding humiliation and personal attacks;
- protecting confidential information;
- listening before responding; and
- remembering that disagreement does not justify disrespect.

This applies in person and online.

---

## We Disagree Without Becoming Divided

A healthy organisation will contain different ideas.

Different opinions.

Different approaches.

And different personalities.

MySAIYa does not require everyone to agree.

It requires us to learn how to disagree responsibly.

We seek to discuss ideas without attacking people.

To listen before assuming.

To clarify before reacting.

To resolve difficulties through appropriate channels.

And to place the wellbeing of the collective above the desire to prove ourselves right.

Unity does not mean uniformity.

It means learning to work together despite our differences.

---

## We Honour Trust

Young adults may encounter personal information through friendships, leadership responsibilities, service activities, registrations, digital platforms and organisational work.

Trust must be protected.

We therefore handle personal and organisational information responsibly.

We do not share confidential information casually.

We respect privacy.

We follow applicable data protection requirements.

We use photographs, recordings and personal stories responsibly.

And we remember that access to information is a responsibility, not a privilege.

---

## We Use Digital Spaces Responsibly

The MySAIYa Digital Hub, social media channels, messaging groups and other online spaces are extensions of our community.

The values expected in physical spaces apply online as well.

We seek to:

- communicate respectfully;
- avoid harassment and bullying;
- protect personal information;
- respect intellectual property;
- avoid spreading misinformation;
- use appropriate language and content;
- obtain necessary permission before sharing photographs or personal stories;
- respect moderation decisions; and
- use technology to strengthen rather than diminish human connection.

Behind every screen is a person.

We communicate accordingly.

---

## We Maintain Appropriate Boundaries

Healthy communities require appropriate personal and organisational boundaries.

Members are expected to behave responsibly and respectfully in their interactions with others.

This includes sensitivity towards:

- personal space;
- interpersonal relationships;
- gender considerations;
- cultural expectations;
- safeguarding;
- privacy; and
- the comfort and dignity of others.

Certain programmes may observe gender-considerate arrangements in accordance with SSIOM practices, programme requirements and the diverse cultural and religious sensitivities of participants.

These practices should always be implemented thoughtfully and respectfully.

---

## We Care for Our Health and Wellbeing

Our ability to serve and contribute is connected to our physical, mental and emotional wellbeing.

We encourage young adults to care responsibly for their health.

To seek appropriate help when needed.

To recognise personal limitations.

To avoid harmful substances and behaviours.

And to develop habits that support a balanced and purposeful life.

Spirituality should help us become more responsible towards life, not less.

---

## We Participate Responsibly

MySAIYa is built by volunteers.

Every contribution matters.

When we accept a responsibility, we seek to honour it.

This means:

- communicating clearly;
- arriving prepared;
- respecting time;
- completing agreed tasks;
- informing others when circumstances change;
- asking for help when necessary; and
- taking responsibility for mistakes.

Volunteering does not mean standards do not matter.

Offering our time freely should inspire us to offer it sincerely.

---

## We Recognise That Leadership Requires Accountability

Those entrusted with leadership responsibilities carry an additional duty of care.

Leaders should seek to:

- act in the interest of the collective;
- use authority responsibly;
- communicate transparently where appropriate;
- avoid conflicts of interest;
- protect confidential information;
- welcome constructive feedback;
- acknowledge mistakes;
- develop others;
- follow organisational processes; and
- remember that leadership is temporary.

Authority within MySAIYa should always remain connected to service.

---

## We Remain Politically Neutral

MySAIYa and SSIOM maintain political neutrality.

Our organisational platforms, programmes and resources must not be used to campaign for, endorse or oppose political parties or candidates.

Individual members may have their own personal views and civic responsibilities.

However, partisan political activity must remain separate from MySAIYa and SSIOM programmes, communications and organisational identity.

Our service is offered without political discrimination.

---

## We Respect the Organisation We Belong To

MySAIYa is an integral part of SSIOM.

Our programmes, communications, finances, data, partnerships and organisational activities must operate within the appropriate SSIOM frameworks, policies and approvals.

Collective discipline is necessary for trust.

Creativity and innovation are encouraged.

But they must be accompanied by responsibility.

We seek not merely to ask:

**Can this be done?**

But also:

**Should it be done?**

**Is it consistent with our values?**

**Does it respect the Organisation?**

**Will it strengthen the collective?**

---

## Our Spiritual Discipline

Members are encouraged to deepen their spiritual lives through sincere personal practice and active participation in the life of their Sai Centre.

The spiritual disciplines encouraged across the Sai movement include practices such as:

- prayer and meditation;
- devotional singing;
- study and reflection;
- participation in selfless service;
- responsible care for health;
- purity in thought, word and action;
- respect for all religions;
- moderation in desires;
- avoidance of harmful substances; and
- regular self-examination.

These practices are not intended as a scorecard for judging others.

Their purpose is self-transformation.

The most important question is not:

**How well is someone else practising?**

It is:

**How sincerely am I trying to grow?**

---

## We Are Accountable First to Ourselves

No document can create character.

No guideline can replace conscience.

No organisation can monitor every decision.

Ultimately, each person must decide how they wish to live.

MySAIYa therefore encourages self-accountability.

To reflect.

To correct ourselves.

To apologise when necessary.

To learn from mistakes.

To begin again.

And to continue striving.

---

## How We Walk Together

We will make mistakes.

We will misunderstand one another.

We will occasionally fall short of our own ideals.

The answer is not to abandon the ideals.

It is to return to them.

Again.

And again.

With greater understanding.

Greater humility.

And greater love.

Because the strength of MySAIYa will never be determined only by the programmes we organise.

It will be determined by the people we become while organising them.

**This is how we seek to walk together.**

**With Sai at the centre.**

**With respect for one another.**

**With responsibility towards the collective.**

**And with a sincere commitment to becoming better than we were yesterday.**
