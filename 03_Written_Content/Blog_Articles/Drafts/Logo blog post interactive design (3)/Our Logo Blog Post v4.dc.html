The story of our logo

One Lotus, Many Paths

The story behind our logo — five human values, five great faiths, and the one religion of love that holds them together.

You have seen it on banners, name tags and the cover of every programme booklet. But our logo is more than a mark of affiliation with the Sri Sathya Sai International Organisation. It is a small map of the whole spiritual journey.

At its centre stands a pillar crowned by a lotus and a flame. Around it bloom five petals. In the original emblem, each petal held the symbol of one of the world's great religions. Today, they carry the five human values every faith shares: Truth, Right Conduct, Peace, Love and Non-violence.

Both versions say the same thing in different words. In Swami's own declaration: there is only one religion, the religion of love. This piece explores the emblem petal by petal — and why, for a community as diverse as Malaysia's, it fits so naturally.

Islam · Ramadhan 1978

A mosque on Sivalayam Street

During the Ramadhan of 1978, Bhagawan inaugurated the mosque He built for the Muslims of Puttaparthi — the Jamia Masjid.

It was not tucked away in some unseen corner of the village. It stands on Sivalayam Street, which also houses the Shiva temple erected at Baba's birthplace. Until then, the Muslims of the village — mostly poor farmers — prayed in a hut that could hold only about 15 worshippers.

Every Ramadhan

Whenever Ramadhan was celebrated, Bhagawan would enlighten His students and devotees on its meaning and significance.

04 · The pillar at the centre

The Sarva Dharma Stupa

The pillar in our logo is real. Called the world religions stupa, it stands near the temple in Prasanthi Nilayam, Bhagawan's main ashram in Puttaparthi — once the front face of Yajur Mandir, His residence.

Did you know?

Not a single contractor laid a brick. The entire Sarva Dharma Aikya Stupa was raised by the bare hands of volunteers — devotees and students alone — under the guidance of Brigadier S. K. Bose, in time for Swami to unveil it on His 50th birthday, 23 November 1975.

In September 1975, Swami handed Brigadier Bose a rather large task with a rather firm deadline: build a landmark of His mission, ready for His birthday just two months away.

First came the foundation — a seven-foot pit, to be filled with soil carried in from a distance. For three days the devotees dug, hauled and tipped until the pit was full and ready for its concrete slabs.

Then, at 3 am on the fourth day, the heavens opened. The Brigadier lay awake the rest of the night, certain that three days of work were being washed away. At first light he hurried to the site to count the damage — and found none. Not a rivulet, not a dent. The soil sat exactly as the volunteers had left it.

When Swami arrived at 10 am on His usual round, the Brigadier met Him with folded palms: “I witnessed an unprecedented Glory of God this morning.” Swami replied gently, “Whenever any work is done by My devotees with full devotion, no damage can come to their work.”

Soon after, the whole Central Trust walked over from a meeting in the Mandir. Swami had the Brigadier mix concrete, then handed a pan-full to every Trust Member — each of whom poured it into the frame themselves. Students from Brindavan and Anantapur took over in relays, pan after pan, and the column was finished in 60 days.

It stands today as a monument to the Unity of Faiths — quite literally concretised by devotion.
Did you know?

Puttaparthi's stupa is technically the third

The first monument to carry the message of unity of faiths was the Lotus Stupa in the grounds of the Shirdi Sai temple in Guindy, Chennai — consecrated and unveiled by Swami in February 1973. That temple also happens to be the very first in the world with an idol of Shirdi Sai Baba. The second, a golden stupa at Dharmakshetra, Mumbai, was unveiled in May 1975. Puttaparthi's followed in November 1975 — and the message has kept travelling since.

06 · Prasanthi Nilayam and beyond

A symbol you cannot miss

Today the Sarva Dharma symbol is synonymous with Prasanthi Nilayam. It greets you on the arch into Puttaparthi, repeats across the blue, white, pink and yellow buildings along the road, and rests on the front face of the marble Mahasamadhi in Sai Kulwant Hall. At the Hill View Stadium, the great masters and prophets stand side by side on the hillside — and far away in Kodaikanal, the emblem crowns the gate of Sai Sruthi, Swami's hill ashram. 07 · Malaysian roots, Sai wings

A logo for every Malaysian

In 1997, the five values logo came to represent the international organisation — speaking beyond any single belief and suiting the religious climate of every country. It fitted Malaysia's multilingual, multiethnic fabric naturally. The Majlis Pusat Sathya Sai Baba Malaysia used a trilingual version from 1997 to 2014. Today, SSIOM carries the clean, crisp logo adopted by all SSSIO-affiliated bodies since 2014.
