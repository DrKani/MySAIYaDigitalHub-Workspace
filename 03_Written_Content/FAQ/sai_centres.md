## About Sai Centres

### What is a Sathya Sai Centre?

A Sathya Sai Centre is a spiritual community where individuals and families come together for spiritual growth, devotional activities, selfless service, human values education and fellowship. It provides a welcoming environment to learn, reflect, serve and put the teachings of Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba into practice in everyday life.

### What is the purpose of a Sai Centre?

A Sai Centre provides opportunities for individuals to deepen their spiritual awareness, practise selfless service, develop human values and grow through fellowship with others. Its activities ultimately encourage personal growth and self-transformation.

### Is a Sai Centre a temple?

A Sai Centre is primarily a spiritual centre rather than a temple. It is a place where people gather for devotional singing, prayer, spiritual study, service, education and other activities inspired by the teachings of Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba.

### Is Sathya Sai a religion?

No. The Sathya Sai movement does not seek to establish a new religion. Its teachings encourage individuals to practise their own faith sincerely while recognising the underlying unity of all religions.

### Do I have to change my religion to participate?

No. You are encouraged to remain sincere to your own faith and deepen your understanding and practice of it. Sai Centres welcome the spirit of interfaith understanding and respect for different paths to the Divine.

### Is the Sathya Sai Organisation interfaith?

Yes. The Organisation brings together people from different racial, cultural and religious backgrounds and promotes mutual respect, unity and the universal Human Values shared across faith traditions.

### Why join a Sai Centre if I can practise spirituality at home?

Personal spiritual practice remains important, but a Sai Centre offers something complementary: good company, collective prayer, opportunities to learn, avenues to serve and a community with whom you can grow. It provides a supportive environment for putting spiritual values into practice.

### Do I need to know about Sathya Sai Baba before visiting?

Not at all. You are welcome whether you have known of Sathya Sai Baba for many years or are simply curious. You can attend, observe, ask questions and discover the activities at your own pace.

### Do I have to participate in every activity?

No. Spiritual growth is personal and self-driven. You may explore the activities that are meaningful to you and participate at a pace with which you are comfortable.

### What can I expect to gain from participating in a Sai Centre?

Sai Centre life provides opportunities to deepen spiritual understanding, practise human values, serve others, develop meaningful friendships and gradually bring spirituality into everyday life.

## Visiting a Sai Centre

### Can anyone visit a Sai Centre?

Sai Centres welcome those who wish to learn about Sathya Sai Baba’s teachings and participate in spiritual, service and educational activities, subject to the Organisation’s applicable membership and participation guidelines. You do not have to become a registered member simply to attend regular activities such as bhajans.

### Do I have to become a member before attending?

No. Membership is not compulsory for attending bhajans and participating in many Centre activities. You may first experience Centre life and consider becoming more actively involved later.

### Can I attend just to see what it is like?

Yes. You can begin by attending an activity, observing and getting to know the community. There is no expectation that you must immediately become a member or take on responsibilities.

### Do Sai Centre activities cost anything?

No fees or subscriptions are charged for participating in Sai Centre activities.

### Am I expected to make a donation?

No. Participation is not dependent upon making a donation, and there is no compulsion to contribute financially.

### How are activities funded if there are no fees?

Activities are supported by voluntary contributions from devotees and well-wishers. Contributions are made voluntarily and discreetly rather than through compulsory fees or public solicitation.

### What should I wear when visiting a Sai Centre?

Simple and modest clothing appropriate for a spiritual gathering is encouraged. There is no need to purchase any special clothing simply to attend.

### Do I have to wear white?

No. White is traditionally preferred for certain spiritual gatherings and symbolises purity, but it is not compulsory.

### Why do many devotees wear white?

White is traditionally associated with purity and simplicity and is therefore commonly worn during spiritual gatherings, particularly bhajans.

### Why do men and women sometimes sit separately?

Separate seating during devotional programmes helps maintain modesty and supports an atmosphere conducive to prayer and inward concentration. It can also be practical when gatherings are crowded.

## Understanding Centre life

### What are the main dimensions of Sai Centre life?

Sai Centre life brings together devotion, spiritual study and selfless service, supported by educational, family, youth and community activities. These are not intended to remain separate compartments; together they support the individual’s journey of self-transformation.

### Why combine devotion, study and service?

Each develops a different dimension of spiritual life. Devotion turns the heart towards the Divine, study deepens understanding and reflection, while service provides an opportunity to put love and spiritual values into action. Together they offer a more integrated approach to spiritual growth.

### Is the goal simply to attend more Sai Centre activities?

No. Participation is a means rather than the final goal. Activities are intended to encourage greater spiritual awareness, more intense practice and ultimately self-transformation and recognition of one’s innate divinity.

### What does “self-transformation” actually mean in Sai Centre life?

Self-transformation means allowing spiritual understanding to influence the way we live — our thoughts, words, actions, relationships and choices. The emphasis moves from simply knowing spiritual teachings towards practising them until they increasingly become part of our character.

### Do I need to become “spiritual” before I start participating?

No. The Centre exists precisely to provide opportunities to learn, practise, serve and grow. Everyone progresses differently, and participation can develop gradually according to one’s own interest and pace.

### Is Sai Centre life meant to affect how I live outside the Centre?

Yes. Spiritual development is ultimately about translating values into everyday life. Prayer, study and service are intended to help strengthen character and influence how we live at home, at work and within society. The MySAIYa framework likewise emphasises integrating Sathya Sai Baba’s teachings into home, work and community life.

### Is there only one “correct” way to begin my Sai journey?

No. Sai Centre life offers many avenues — devotion, study, service, education, family programmes, youth activities and fellowship. You can begin with the activities that resonate most with you and explore others gradually.

### What if I prefer service to devotional activities?

Service is itself an important spiritual avenue within Sai Centre life. You may begin by serving and gradually explore other dimensions such as devotional activities or spiritual study as your interests develop.

### What if I prefer quiet reflection and study rather than singing?

That is perfectly fine. Bhajans are only one avenue of spiritual practice. Study Circles, reading, reflection, self-transformation programmes and service provide other ways of engaging with Sai teachings.

## Faiths, prayers & traditions

### Why are prayers at a Sai Centre drawn from different religions?

Sai Centres recognise the underlying unity of all faiths. Devotional programmes may therefore include prayers, songs or names of the Divine associated with different religious traditions. The intention is not to combine religions into a new faith, but to cultivate respect for the many paths through which people approach the same Divine.

### Are festivals from different religions celebrated at Sai Centres?

Yes. The interfaith spirit of the Sai movement encourages respect for the sacred occasions of different religions. Sai Centres may observe major religious festivals as opportunities to understand their spiritual significance and appreciate the unity underlying different faith traditions.

### Why does a Sai Centre sometimes look Hindu if the Organisation is interfaith?

In Malaysia, many Sai devotees have historically come from an Indian or Hindu background, so some familiar practices and visual traditions naturally reflect that heritage. This is cultural context rather than a requirement that participants become Hindu. In other parts of the world, Sai Centres may look and practise differently according to their local communities.

### Why are practices such as Arati and Vibhuti distribution found at some Sai Centres?

Some practices reflect the cultural and devotional traditions familiar to the local community. Their spiritual value lies not merely in performing a ritual, but in the love, devotion and righteousness with which it is performed. Such practices can also differ between Sai Centres and countries.

### Do all Sai Centres around the world look and function exactly the same way?

No. While Sai Centres share the same spiritual foundation and values, their cultural expressions can differ according to the communities and countries in which they operate. Practices common in a predominantly Indian community, for example, may not be common in a Centre elsewhere in the world.

### Why are pictures of Sathya Sai Baba displayed at Sai Centres?

Many devotees relate to Sathya Sai Baba as their spiritual teacher, Guru, Avatar or chosen form of the Divine. His image therefore naturally holds an important place in many Sai Centres and serves as a focus for devotion and remembrance.

### Why might I see a picture of Shirdi Sai Baba as well?

Sathya Sai Baba identified Shirdi Sai Baba as His previous incarnation. For this reason, images of both Shirdi Sai Baba and Sathya Sai Baba are commonly found in Sai Centres.

### Must every Sai Centre have pictures of Shirdi Sai Baba and Sathya Sai Baba on its altar?

No. Although their images are commonly displayed, they are not mandatory on every Sai Centre’s altar.

### Why do devotees call Sathya Sai Baba “Swami” or “Bhagawan”?

Devotees relate to Sathya Sai Baba in different ways, including as a spiritual teacher, Guru, Avatar or the Divine. Titles such as Swami and Bhagawan reflect this devotional relationship. A visitor does not need to adopt any particular form of address or understanding before participating.

### Do I have to believe that Sathya Sai Baba is God to visit a Sai Centre?

No. You may relate to Sathya Sai Baba as a spiritual teacher, Avatar or God according to your own understanding. You do not need to adopt a particular belief before visiting a Sai Centre.

### What is the difference between religion and spirituality in the Sai context?

Religion generally provides a particular tradition, philosophy, practices and forms of worship. Spirituality concerns the deeper inner experience of seeking the Divine, developing higher consciousness and transforming oneself. Sai Centres emphasise this spiritual foundation while encouraging individuals to remain sincere to their own faith.

### Does being interfaith mean that all religions are treated as identical?

No. Different religions may have different philosophies, practices and approaches. The Sai approach respects these differences while recognising an underlying spiritual unity and a shared call towards love and the Divine.

### Is conversion part of the Sai movement?

No. The Sai movement is non-proselytising. Sathya Sai Baba encouraged people to practise their own religion sincerely rather than abandon it for another faith.

### If I already practise my own religion seriously, is there still a place for me at a Sai Centre?

Yes. Sai teachings encourage people to deepen, rather than replace, sincere religious practice. The Centre provides additional opportunities for spiritual reflection, selfless service, Human Values and fellowship while respecting one’s own faith tradition.

## Bhajans & devotional activities

### What is a bhajan?

A bhajan is a devotional song sung in praise of God. At Sai Centres, bhajans are generally sung collectively in a call-and-response format, allowing everyone to participate in the devotional experience.

### Are Sai Centres mainly for bhajans?

No. Bhajans are an important part of Centre life, but they are only one aspect of it. Sai Centres also provide opportunities for spiritual study, reflection, selfless service, human values education, self-transformation and community fellowship.

### Why are bhajans important?

Collective devotional singing helps turn the mind towards the Divine and creates an atmosphere of devotion, harmony and joy. Singing together can also foster a powerful sense of unity among participants.

### What if I don’t know any bhajans?

That’s perfectly fine. You can listen, follow the group and gradually become familiar with the songs. You do not need to know the bhajans before attending.

### What if I don’t understand the language being sung?

You can still participate. Bhajans are intended to create devotion and unity beyond language, and you may gradually explore the meaning of individual songs as you become familiar with them.

### Are bhajans only sung in Indian languages?

No. Although many familiar Sai bhajans have Indian origins, devotional songs may be sung in different languages, reflecting the universal and interfaith spirit of the movement.

### What makes a good Sai bhajan?

A good bhajan combines devotion with suitable melody, rhythm and sweetness. More importantly, it should help turn the minds of those singing and listening towards the Divine.

### Why is understanding the meaning of a bhajan important?

Understanding what we sing allows devotion to come from both the heart and the mind. Lead singers in particular are encouraged to understand the meaning and devotional significance of the songs they lead.

### Can bhajans be sung at home?

Yes. Devotional singing need not be limited to the Sai Centre. Individual prayer, family bhajans and other appropriate forms of devotional singing are encouraged.

### What is Namasmarana?

Namasmarana means remembering or repeating the Name of God. It can take the form of silent remembrance, chanting or devotional singing and is practised as a way of keeping the mind centred on the Divine.

### What spiritual activities are available besides bhajans?

Depending on the Centre, activities may include prayer, spiritual reading, reflection, Study Circles and programmes aimed at personal and spiritual development.

## Study Circles

### What is a Study Circle?

A Study Circle is a participatory spiritual discussion in which a group explores a teaching or topic from different perspectives. Rather than simply acquiring information, participants reflect on what the teaching means and how it can be applied in everyday life.

### Is a Study Circle the same as a lecture?

No. A Study Circle is intended to be participatory. Everyone should have an opportunity to reflect, listen and contribute rather than simply receiving information from one speaker.

### Do I need extensive spiritual knowledge to join a Study Circle?

No. Study Circles are opportunities to learn together. Participants bring different experiences and perspectives, and everyone should be given an opportunity to contribute.

### Can people have different opinions during a Study Circle?

Yes. Different perspectives are welcome, but discussion should remain respectful and non-argumentative. The purpose is to deepen understanding rather than prove another participant wrong.

### Who leads a Study Circle?

A facilitator guides the session, encourages participation, keeps the discussion focused and helps maintain a respectful environment.

### Can members take turns presenting Study Circle topics?

Yes. Giving participants opportunities to prepare and present topics can strengthen confidence, study skills and personal engagement with the teachings.

### How often are Study Circles held?

Study Circles are generally held monthly, with sessions lasting approximately one hour. Contact your local Centre for its current schedule.

### Is spiritual study mainly about learning more information?

No. Knowledge becomes meaningful when it is reflected upon and applied. Spiritual study therefore places importance on how a teaching can influence one’s thoughts, choices, relationships and daily life.

## Selfless service

### Why is service such an important part of Sai Centre life?

Selfless service is a practical expression of love. It provides an opportunity to help those in need while cultivating compassion, humility, gratitude and a greater awareness of our connection with others.

### Is Sai service simply volunteering?

It goes beyond completing a voluntary task. Service performed without seeking recognition or reward is regarded as a spiritual practice — an opportunity to reduce self-centredness and allow love to be expressed through action.

### How can serving others help transform me?

Service shifts attention from “me” towards “we”. By recognising the needs and dignity of others, we cultivate gratitude, compassion and humility while gradually reducing selfishness and ego.

### I’ve never volunteered before. Can I still participate?

Absolutely. You do not need previous experience to begin serving. More experienced volunteers can guide newcomers and help them become comfortable with the activity.

### What types of service activities are carried out?

Service activities can broadly include Sociocare, Medicare, Educare, Animal Care and Environment Care, providing different ways for volunteers to contribute according to community needs and their own abilities.

### What is Sociocare?

Sociocare focuses on supporting people and communities in need. Activities may include helping needy families, visiting older persons or hospital patients, improving homes and communities and participating in relief efforts.

### What is Medicare?

Medicare activities use healthcare as an avenue of service. Examples include medical camps, specialist services, dental and eye care, health screenings and blood donation drives.

### What is Educare?

Educare-related service supports education and the development of Human Values. This may include Education in Human Values initiatives, youth programmes and ParentCare activities.

### Does Sai service include caring for animals?

Yes. Animal Care can include supporting animal shelters, helping maintain their facilities and caring for animals in need.

### Are there environmental service activities?

Yes. Environment Care may include tree planting, beach cleaning, recycling, reuse initiatives and activities that promote greater environmental awareness.

### Are medical camps and blood donation programmes part of Sai service?

Yes. Healthcare-related service, including medical camps, health screenings and blood donation initiatives, forms part of our service activities.

### How do I find out what service activities my local Sai Centre is currently doing?

Service opportunities vary according to local needs and the activities of each Centre. Check with your local Sai Centre contact and look out for current announcements and volunteer opportunities.

### Do I need professional skills to volunteer?

Not necessarily. Sai service includes many different forms of participation, and experienced volunteers can guide newcomers. What matters most is the willingness to serve sincerely and responsibly.

### Can I contribute my professional skills through a Sai Centre?

Yes. Depending on local needs and programmes, you may be able to contribute skills in healthcare, education, environmental care, community improvement, event management or other areas of service. Speak with your Centre about where your abilities could help.

### Why is mentoring important when newcomers join service activities?

A newcomer may be enthusiastic but unsure about what to do. Experienced members can provide guidance, companionship and practical support so that service becomes both meaningful and enjoyable.

### Is service meant to make me feel good about helping someone less fortunate?

The deeper emphasis is on transformation rather than recognition or personal importance. Selfless service encourages us to reduce ego, develop compassion and recognise the opportunity to grow through serving another person.

### Can service be enjoyable?

Yes. Serving with others can create friendship, teamwork and a genuine sense of fulfilment. The spirit is sincere service without reward, but that does not mean the experience cannot also be joyful.

### Does service extend beyond helping people?

Yes. Sai service extends to society, healthcare, education, animals and the environment. Caring for nature and other living beings is therefore part of the broader understanding of service.

### What does caring for the environment have to do with spirituality?

Care for nature is an expression of responsibility and reverence for life. Environmental service can include conserving resources, reducing pollution, recycling, reusing, tree planting and caring for shared natural spaces.

## Children & Sai Spiritual Education

### Are there activities for children at Sai Centres?

Yes. Sai Spiritual Education (SSE) provides children with structured opportunities to develop character, spiritual awareness and Human Values.

### What is Sai Spiritual Education (SSE)?

SSE is a values-based spiritual education programme designed to nurture character and help children recognise and practise Human Values in everyday life.

### What Human Values are taught in SSE?

The five fundamental Human Values are Truth, Right Conduct, Peace, Love and Non-Violence.

### What is SSE trying to develop in children?

SSE seeks to nurture noble character, self-confidence, self-discipline, responsibility and inner strength so that children are better equipped to face life’s challenges while living according to Human Values.

### How is SSE different from ordinary academic education?

Academic education largely prepares children with knowledge and skills for life and livelihood. SSE complements this by focusing on character, values and the development of the whole person.

### How are children taught in SSE?

Learning may include silent sitting, storytelling, positive affirmations, music and group activities, making values education engaging and experiential rather than purely theoretical.

### Who conducts SSE classes?

Classes are conducted by trained SSE gurus using structured lessons designed to nurture spiritual understanding and character.

### What does Head, Heart and Hands mean?

The Head, Heart and Hands approach encourages harmony between what we think, what we feel and what we do. It reflects the importance of unity in thought, word and deed.

### Does SSE teach practical life skills too?

Yes. Its objectives include developing self-confidence, self-reliance, self-discipline, problem-solving, decision-making, communication and social skills alongside spiritual and character development.

### Does SSE teach children to care for nature?

Yes. Reverence and care for nature, including plants and animals, forms part of the values and attitudes SSE seeks to nurture.

### Is spiritual education only for children?

No. While SSE provides structured education for children, the wider principle of spiritual education and self-transformation extends to teenagers, young adults and adults as well.

### Does Sai education tell children what to believe?

Its emphasis is on helping children recognise and experience Human Values for themselves and develop noble character. The programme centres on Truth, Right Conduct, Peace, Love and Non-Violence.

### Is SSE only about learning stories and prayers?

No. Its aims extend to self-confidence, self-reliance, self-discipline, problem-solving, decision-making, communication, social skills, harmonious living and unity of thought, word and deed.

### What does it mean to educate the “Head, Heart and Hands”?

It means developing the whole person so that thought, feeling and action work in harmony. In practical terms, spiritual understanding should not remain merely intellectual; it should be felt within and expressed through conduct.

## Families & parents

### Is a Sai Centre suitable for families?

Yes. Centre life can involve children, young people, adults and parents. Beyond formal programmes, it provides opportunities for families to build friendships, participate together and become part of a wider spiritual community.

### Is there support for parents?

Yes. The Sathya Sai ParentCare Programme is designed to help parents explore their responsibilities and challenges while drawing upon Human Values and inner wisdom in family life.

### What is ParentCare?

ParentCare uses an “Inside Out Approach” to help participants draw upon their inner wisdom when responding to parenting and everyday-life challenges.

### Is ParentCare only for parents?

No. ParentCare welcomes both parents and young adults. The principles explored can support family relationships and personal development more broadly.

### Is everything at a Sai Centre formal or devotional?

No. Community life can also include outings, dinners, movies, bowling, hiking, picnics, celebrations and other opportunities to enjoy one another’s company and strengthen friendships.

### Can a Sai Centre really become a “home away from home”?

It can. Sai Centres bring people together not only for programmes but also for friendship, fellowship and mutual support. This can be especially meaningful for students and others living away from their families.

## Young people

### Is there a place for young people in Sai Centres?

Absolutely. Young people are encouraged to participate actively in spiritual activities, service, sports, leadership development and community programmes.

### What kinds of activities are available for young people?

Activities can include spiritual and values-based programmes, service projects, sports, leadership development and opportunities to contribute their talents to Centre and community initiatives.

### Can I develop leadership skills through Sai activities?

Yes. Opportunities may help develop skills such as public speaking, facilitation, people management, event management and technical areas such as sound and lighting.

### Are there sports and recreational activities?

Yes. Examples include futsal, badminton and handball, alongside other social and community activities.

### Can young people participate in community service?

Yes. Young volunteers can participate in a wide variety of service activities, from supporting vulnerable communities to serving people with disabilities and caring for animals.

### I’m studying away from home. Can I still get involved?

Yes. A Sai Centre can provide friendship, spiritual companionship and an extended family environment — a “home away from home” while you study or work away from your family.

### Are young adults simply participants, or can they help lead activities?

Young adults are encouraged to contribute their energy, talents, expertise and ideas. Opportunities can extend across spiritual, service, educational, cultural and leadership activities, allowing young adults both to participate and to take responsibility.

### What can a Sai Centre offer me as a young adult besides bhajans?

Young adults can find opportunities for service, introspection, devotional practice, leadership development, cultural understanding, skill development and community. These experiences are intended to support purposeful living at home, at work and in society.

### Can I use my talents rather than simply attend programmes?

Yes. Young adults are encouraged to contribute their expertise, energy, talents and ideas. Sai activities can therefore become spaces where creative, organisational, technical, leadership and professional abilities are channelled towards meaningful purposes.

### Does Sai Centre involvement help develop practical skills?

It can. Activities provide opportunities to develop skills such as public speaking, facilitation, people management, event management and technical support while serving a larger purpose.

### How does MySAIYa relate to my local Sai Centre?

MySAIYa is the Malaysian Sai Young Adults community within SSIOM. For young adults aged 18 to 40, it provides wider opportunities for spiritual, service, educational, cultural and leadership development while remaining connected to the life and structure of the wider Sai Organisation.

### Do I have to choose between being involved in MySAIYa and my Sai Centre?

No. They complement each other. Your Sai Centre provides your local spiritual community, while MySAIYa connects young adults to wider opportunities, initiatives and friendships across the Malaysian Sai community. MySAIYa operates within SSIOM rather than as a separate organisation.

## Personal & spiritual growth

### What is the Self Transformation Programme (STP)?

STP is a voluntary programme designed to encourage personal spiritual practice and self-transformation. It incorporates the Code of Conduct, spiritual networking, peer support and facilitated workshops.

### Is the Self Transformation Programme compulsory?

No. Participation is voluntary.

### What does “Educare” mean?

Educare is based on the idea that true education is not merely about bringing information in from outside, but drawing out the values and wisdom already present within the individual.

### What does self-transformation mean?

Self-transformation is the gradual awakening and expression of the love and goodness already within us. It is reflected not merely in what we know, but in how we think, speak, act and relate to others.

### What are the five aims of transformational education?

They are Knowledge, Skill, Balance, Insight and Identity — representing progressive dimensions of learning and personal transformation.

## Spiritual practice & the 9-Point Code of Conduct

### What is the 9-Point Code of Conduct?

The 9-Point Code of Conduct is a practical framework for bringing spirituality into everyday life through prayer, devotional practice, service, spiritual study, loving speech and responsible living.

### Does the Code of Conduct encourage daily prayer or meditation?

Yes. Daily meditation and prayer according to one’s own religious tradition forms part of the Code.

### Does it encourage spiritual practice as a family?

Yes. Weekly devotional singing and prayer with family members is encouraged.

### Does the Code encourage community service?

Yes. Participation in community work and the Organisation’s service programmes forms part of the Code.

### Does it encourage spiritual reading?

Yes. Regular study of Sai literature is encouraged as part of ongoing spiritual development.

### What does the Code teach about how we speak to others?

It encourages us to speak softly and lovingly to everyone and to avoid speaking ill of others, particularly in their absence.

### What is “Ceiling on Desires”?

Ceiling on Desires is a spiritual discipline that encourages us to place sensible limits on our wants and redirect the resulting savings towards service to others.

## Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba

### Who is Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba?

Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba was born in Puttaparthi, Andhra Pradesh, India, on 23 November 1926. From a young age, He dedicated His life to a spiritual mission centred on the transformation of the individual through love, selfless service and the practice of Human Values.

### What is the central message of Sathya Sai Baba’s teachings?

A central teaching is that our true nature is divine and that the purpose of life is to recognise and manifest that divinity through love, right living, spiritual practice and service.

### What are the five Human Values?

The five fundamental Human Values are Truth (Sathya), Right Conduct (Dharma), Peace (Shanti), Love (Prema) and Non-Violence (Ahimsa).

### Did Sathya Sai Baba ask people to abandon their own religion?

No. He encouraged people to practise their own religion sincerely and strengthen their own faith rather than change religions.

### How should I understand Sathya Sai Baba — as a teacher, Guru or God?

People relate to Sathya Sai Baba in different ways. Some regard Him as a spiritual teacher or Guru, while devotees may recognise Him as an Avatar or the Divine. Each person is free to develop their own understanding.

### What did Sathya Sai Baba teach about different religions?

He taught respect for all religions and emphasised their underlying spiritual unity while recognising that traditions may differ in their philosophies, practices and forms of worship.

### What is the relationship between Shirdi Sai Baba and Sathya Sai Baba?

Sathya Sai Baba identified Shirdi Sai Baba as His previous incarnation, describing Shirdi Sai as His “previous body.”

### What about the miracles associated with Sathya Sai Baba?

Miracles and manifestations became widely associated with Sathya Sai Baba, but He consistently placed greater importance on His spiritual message and the transformation of the individual. He described such phenomena as a “calling card” that could initially draw people towards His teachings.

### When did Sathya Sai Baba leave His physical body?

Bhagawan Sri Sathya Sai Baba left His physical body on 24 April 2011.

## Membership & organisation

### What is SSIOM?

SSIOM is the Sathya Sai International Organisation of Malaysia, an interfaith, non-profit organisation dedicated to the promotion of Human Values, spiritual development and selfless service.

### When was SSIOM established in Malaysia?

The Sathya Sai Organisation has been formally established in Malaysia since 1983.

### How are Sai Centres connected to SSIOM?

Sai Centres operate as branches within the Sathya Sai International Organisation of Malaysia and form the local community through which many of its spiritual, educational and service activities are conducted.

### Do I have to become a member to participate?

No. You can attend bhajans and participate in many Centre activities without immediately becoming a registered member. Those who later wish to take a more active role may consider membership.

### Are there membership fees?

No membership fee or subscription is charged.

### Is service part of being a Sai member?

Service is an important aspect of Sai life. Members are encouraged to make a personal commitment to serving society, with the emphasis placed on sincere voluntary service rather than compulsion.

### Is my local Sai Centre an independent organisation?

No. Sai Centres operate within the organisational framework of SSIOM rather than as unrelated independent groups.

### Who organises the different activities at a Sai Centre?

Alongside the Centre’s office bearers, coordinators and volunteers take responsibility for different activities and areas of Centre life. This allows spiritual, educational, service and other programmes to be organised by teams rather than resting on one individual.

### Can ordinary members help organise Centre activities?

Yes. Sai Centre life depends upon members contributing their time, abilities and willingness to serve. Becoming more involved can include helping with programmes, service activities and other Centre responsibilities according to one’s interest and readiness.

### Do I need an official position before I can contribute?

No. Meaningful participation does not depend upon holding a title. There are many ways to contribute through service, programmes and activities, while formal office-bearing and coordinator roles carry particular organisational responsibilities.

### Why doesn’t the Sai Organisation openly solicit donations?

Fundraising is approached with considerable restraint. Activities are intended to remain accessible without fees, and voluntary contributions are handled discreetly so that spiritual participation is not centred around money or financial pressure.

### Will I be pressured to contribute money once I start attending?

No. There is no compulsion to donate, and participation in Centre activities is not dependent upon financial contribution.

## Community & belonging

### Can I make friends at a Sai Centre?

Absolutely. Fellowship is an important part of Centre life. Sai Centres bring together people who pray, learn, serve and spend time together, allowing genuine friendships to develop naturally.

### Is there room for social activities too?

Yes. Centre life can include outings, dinners, movies, bowling, hiking, picnics and celebrations alongside spiritual, educational and service activities.

### Is a Sai Centre only for very spiritual people?

No. People are at different stages of their spiritual journey. A Sai Centre is a place to learn and grow rather than a community of people claiming to have already reached perfection.

### What if I notice that some members don’t always live up to Sai’s teachings?

Every person is at a different stage of spiritual development. Sai Centre life encourages us to practise tolerance, understanding and acceptance, to recognise the good in others and, above all, to concentrate on our own transformation.

### I’m shy and don’t know anyone. Will I fit in?

You do not need to arrive knowing anyone or understanding everything. Begin simply: attend an activity, observe, meet a few people and participate when you feel comfortable. The purpose of the Centre is to provide a supportive spiritual community in which people can grow at their own pace.

### What’s the easiest way to begin?

Start with whatever feels most natural to you. Attend a bhajan, join a service activity, sit in on a Study Circle or simply visit your nearest Sai Centre and meet the community. There is no need to do everything at once — come, experience and grow at your own pace.
